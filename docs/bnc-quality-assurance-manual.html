BNC Operating Philosophy

BNC is committed to a quality management system that makes quality a basic business principle. Our strategy is based on customer satisfaction, achieved by meeting our internal and our customers’ requirements on time.

Conformance to our customers’ requirements and expectations is the responsibility of every employee at BNC.

Quality assurance systems, procedures, and practices are developed, reviewed, and changed with the participation of employees in a continuous-improvement effort.

BNC’s goal is to continue meeting world-class standards for the mutual benefit of our customers and employees.

Management Policy Statement

It is the policy of BNC management to fully support and provide the necessary resources for continual implementation of the Quality Assurance System.

Management at all levels participates in quality assurance activities as incorporated into daily functional requirements.

No product or service is provided to the customer until its quality and conformance to customer specifications can be assured.

Management assesses the effectiveness of the quality system on a regular basis and directs internal efforts toward continual improvement.

BNC management is committed to the Quality Assurance System outlined in this manual and its supporting documents, and ensures this policy is communicated, understood, implemented, and maintained at all levels of the organization.

General

Foreword

BNC is a distributor and manufacturer of radiation detection systems and test-and-measurement products.

This Quality Assurance Manual (QAM) describes the Quality System implemented at BNC.

The objective of the Quality System is to prevent nonconformances throughout contract performance, provide for the prompt detection of nonconformances, and provide timely and effective corrective action.

The Quality System, designed in conjunction with managerial functions, establishes an effective and economical system for assuring quality.

It is BNC policy to provide full compliance with the Quality System throughout all phases of contract performance and to ensure that only acceptable products and services are provided to the customer.

The system outlined in this manual and its supporting procedures provides compliance with applicable portions of ASME NQA-1 and recognized industry best practices.

Note: These policies are implemented using project-specific plans when specifically identified and stated as part of a contract in non-general terms and related to the products or services on that contract.

1.0 Organization

1A POLICY

Management at Berkeley Nucleonics has documented its commitment to quality and to the support of the Quality Assurance System, communicated throughout the organization to all levels of personnel.

Management continually reviews in-house verification requirements, ensuring the necessary resources are available, including the training and qualification of personnel assigned to verification activities.

2.0 Quality Assurance Program

2A POLICY

BNC has established and documented a Quality Assurance System in compliance with applicable portions of ASME NQA-1, administered through written policy, procedures, and instructions.

Operational procedures are developed for functional activities that affect product or service quality. Quality records provide objective evidence of compliance and measure the effectiveness of the system.

A system is established for the training of personnel and the acquisition of skills, and for identifying training needs.

2B PROCEDURE — QAP-02, QUALITY ASSURANCE PROGRAM

1.0 Purpose

1.1 To describe the type and availability of Quality Assurance documentation at BNC.

2.0 Scope

2.1 Documented procedures and instructions developed for controlling and assuring quality.

3.0 Quality Assurance Documentation

Quality Assurance Manual (QAM) — controlled document describing the Quality System, management policy, organization and responsibilities, the NQA-1 element descriptions, and the QAPs; controlled by the Quality Manager.

Quality Assurance Procedures (QAPs) — controlled documents providing procedures for each applicable NQA-1 element, with references to forms and to other procedures.

Skills Descriptions — skill title/description, requirements, and certification/recertification requirements.

Personnel Files — name, work and training history, current position, and skills acquired.

Training — initiated at the department level, conducted continually; new employees are trained to their position including Quality System familiarization; the Quality Manager qualifies employees performing direct inspection. Records are kept in the personnel file.

7.0 Control of Purchased Items & Services

7A POLICY

The procurement of items and services is controlled to assure conformance with specified requirements. Suppliers and subcontractors are initially qualified and reviewed thereafter at intervals consistent with the nature of the product and the source’s demonstrated performance.

7B PROCEDURE — QAP-07, CONTROL OF PURCHASED ITEMS & SERVICES

1.0 Purpose

1.1 To describe the procurement methods related to subcontractor and supplier control.

2.0 Scope

2.1 The procurement functions related to subcontractor/supplier control.

3.0 Supplier Evaluation and Selection

Prior to award, BNC evaluates the supplier’s capability per the procurement documents. Evaluation is documented and includes one or more of:

Initial survey via the Initial Quality Survey Checklist. Any vendor that can supply an item as specified by manufacturer and model number, designated by Engineering, is automatically approved.

Direct evaluation of facilities, personnel, and quality program; or a mail-out survey.

Satisfactory history of an identical or similar product; customer-directed sources.

Third-party registration (e.g., ISO 9000) — placed on the AVL and exempt from annual audits unless QA deems necessary.

Certification of conformance; OEM, OEM-approved, or OEM-recommended suppliers; objectively-evaluable quality records.

4.0 Bid Evaluation

4.1 When bids are solicited, evaluation includes the supplier’s capability to meet technical and quality requirements; unacceptable conditions are resolved before award.

5.0 Control of Supplier-Generated Documents

5.1 Controls provide for the acquisition, processing, and recorded evaluation of supplier documentation against acceptance criteria.

6.0 Acceptance of Item or Service

6.1 The supplier verifies compliance before acceptance. Where required by code, regulation, or contract, documentary evidence of conformance is available at the site prior to use. Methods include Certificate of Conformance, source verification, receiving inspection, and post-installation testing.

7.0 Approved Vendor List (AVL)

Only approved suppliers are placed on the AVL by QA and Purchasing; QA approves all subcontractors and suppliers.

The AVL details supplier name and address, approved commodities/services, and approval date and signatures.

Suppliers may be removed for lack of conformance or as a corrective-action decision; Government/customer-approved sources are noted on the AVL.

8.0 Subcontractor Control

Subcontractors failing the minimum requirements must upgrade before further procurement; an on-site audit may be required, failing which they are removed from the AVL.

Quality audits are recorded; subcontractors may be audited more frequently as a corrective-action decision.

Surveillance or source inspection is performed at the subcontractor’s plant as required; test data and calibration records are provided to BNC.

9.0 Counterfeit Parts Policy

BNC has implemented a Counterfeit Parts Policy (Form CPP-100), communicated and maintained at all levels.

BNC sources components directly from the manufacturer, approved representatives, or authorized distributors, and requires suppliers to maintain a counterfeit-parts policy.

For hard-to-find or obsolete components, BNC purchases from the least-risk source and inspects or tests on receipt (see also QAP-19).

BNC retains purchase and receiving documentation for OEM traceability. Suspected counterfeit parts are quarantined and reported to the supplier, Quality Manager, and President.

8.0 Identification & Control of Items

8A POLICY

A system provides product identification and control of items throughout processing and delivery. Unique numbering originating from the manufacturer identifies products and provides traceability to raw material/components, date of processing, and inspections/tests performed.

8B PROCEDURE — QAP-08, IDENTIFICATION & CONTROL OF ITEMS

1.0 Purpose

1.1 To describe the methods used to provide identification and control of items.

2.0 Scope

2.1 Product and material identification and control.

3.0 Procedure

3.1 Material and product control is achieved using identifying markings on individual products.

3.2 In-process material is assigned to a Sales Order/job number, which becomes the identification number traceable to the materials and product used.

3.3 All forms and reports generated during processing, inspection, and testing are traceable to the Sales Order number.

10.0 Inspection

10A POLICY

Controls verify material and product upon delivery; the nature and amount of receiving inspection are pre-determined by supplier controls, criticality, and the objective evidence provided with the order. Incoming material is withheld from inventory pending verification.

In-process inspection follows a documented Inspection Plan; final inspection completes the evidence of conformance. Records are maintained, and hold areas segregate nonconforming material.

10B PROCEDURE — QAP-10, INSPECTION

1.0 Purpose

1.1 To describe the methods used for inspection of purchased product.

2.0 Scope

2.1 Receiving, in-process, and final inspection.

3.0 Receiving Inspection

3.1 Receivables are placed in a Hold Area and verified visually against the Packing List for quantity, type, and freedom from damage.

3.2 The receiver compiles the Packing List, Receipt-to-Inventory List, and supplied documents for QA review.

3.3 Product is inspected per the PO notes or an Inspection Instruction Sheet; acceptable material is marked and moved to inventory; unacceptable material is segregated and handled per QAP-15.

4.0 In-Process Inspection

4.1 Mandatory hold points are indicated in the appropriate documents; QA and operators inspect per the Inspection Plan or Work Instruction Sheets.

4.2 QA signs Green Acceptable Tags for accepted material and audits in-process activities; nonconforming product is tagged with a Red Rejection Tag and held for action per QAP-15.

5.0 Final Inspection

5.1 All finished products are subject to final inspection by QA verification and customer acceptance per the Inspection & Test Plan.

5.2 Upon acceptance the product is released; items failing final inspection are handled per QAP-15.

6.0 Post-Shipment Inspection

6.1 Upon a post-shipment finding of concern, BNC provides immediate customer notification and initiates the recall process and product evaluation.

11.0 Test Control

Tests to collect data, verify conformance of an item or computer program, or demonstrate satisfactory performance are planned and executed. Characteristics and methods are specified in procedures; results are documented and evaluated against acceptance criteria.

12.0 Control of Measuring & Test Equipment

12A POLICY

Procedures assure that inspection, measuring, and test equipment (including ancillary software) are controlled and maintained, calibrated to a specified accuracy traceable to NIST. When a device is found out of calibration, the impact on previously-inspected items is evaluated and documented with authorized dispositions.

12B PROCEDURE — QAP-12, CONTROL OF MEASURING & TEST EQUIPMENT

1.0 Purpose

1.1 To detail the requirements for the control of inspection, measuring, and testing equipment.

2.0 Scope

2.1 Control, maintenance, and calibration of all such equipment, including masters.

3.0 Measurement System (General)

3.1 BNC maintains a measurement and calibration system; employee-owned equipment is subject to the same controls as company-owned equipment.

4.0 Adequacy & Environmental Controls

4.1 Equipment has the necessary accuracy, stability, and range for its intended use.

4.2 Equipment is calibrated and used in environments controlled for temperature and humidity; correction factors are applied when required.

5.0 Calibration Intervals, Sources & Records

5.1 Intervals are established per equipment type and adjusted when results warrant; equipment is recalibrated earlier when damaged or suspect.

5.2 All equipment is calibrated against a NIST-traceable standard; accuracy is supported by a certificate or data available on request.

5.3 A calibration-history record is maintained for each item, including identification, last calibration, results, and interval.

6.0 Calibration Labeling & Recall

6.1 Equipment is labeled with the calibration date and due date; all tools and gauges are permanently serial-numbered as required.

6.2 A calendar recall system, maintained by QA and the Operations Manager, ensures timely recalibration or removal from use.

7.0 Out-of-Calibration

7.1 An NCR is raised and QA investigates usage since the last calibration.

7.2 Product is re-inspected with calibrated equipment; if suspect product may have shipped, customers are notified. Equipment is tagged “out of service” and repaired, recalibrated, or replaced.

5.0 ESD Precautions (Electronic Assemblies)

BNC follows best practices to prevent electrostatic-discharge (ESD) damage in assembly and testing, including:

Handle PCB assemblies only at designated work areas; store unused assemblies and sensitive components in shielded bags or boxes.

Wear a grounded wrist strap; handle PCBs by the edges; avoid touching components, leads, solder joints, pins, and exposed circuitry.

Avoid stacking PCB assemblies and limit movement to reduce static build-up.

Before installing a device, touch it (in its antistatic bag) to unpainted system metal for at least two seconds, then install directly.

Take additional care in cold weather, when lower humidity increases static electricity.

16.0 Corrective & Preventive Action

16A POLICY

Procedures assure that conditions adverse to quality are promptly detected, identified, and corrected, and that action precludes recurrence. Customer-presented deficiencies receive timely corrective action; actions are reviewed and monitored for effectiveness internally and at supplier locations, and records are maintained.

16B PROCEDURE — QAP-16, CORRECTIVE & PREVENTIVE ACTION

1.0 Purpose

1.1 To describe the system for providing corrective and preventive action.

2.0 Scope

2.1 Corrective action required to correct conditions adverse to quality.

3.0 General

Corrective actions are initiated from customer requests, NCRs, internal corrective-action requests, and internal audit observations, using a three-step process:

Short-term corrective action

Long-term corrective action (where applicable)

Corrective-action review

4.0 In-House Product Nonconformances

4.1 Root causes are recorded on the NCR; the responsible Department Manager details the corrective action and implementation date and returns the NCR to the Quality Manager, who accepts and monitors effectiveness.

5.0 Audit Nonconformances

5.1 Recorded on the NCR; Department Managers provide corrective action, and the auditor or QA re-audits after action is taken.

6.0 Customer Complaints

6.1 Handled as internal nonconformances; the customer receives a copy of the completed NCR on request. When nonconforming items may have shipped, the Quality Manager notifies the customer and re-inspects affected inventory.

7.0 Supplier Corrective Action

7.1 Suppliers are controlled per QAP-07 and must provide corrective action for nonconforming material or audit observations.

8.0 Corrective Action Sign-Off

8.1 QA expedites corrective action and reviews completed actions for completeness and effectiveness.

4.0 Procedure

4.1 Records provide objective evidence of compliance and are reviewed by QA; documents are Quality Assurance Records only if stamped, initialed or signed, and dated by authorized personnel.

4.2 Records are classified Lifetime or Nonpermanent. Lifetime records are of significant value in demonstrating safe operation; maintaining or modifying an item; determining the cause of a malfunction; or providing baseline data for in-service inspection.

5.0 Identification & Storage

5.1 All forms maintain individual numbering; records are stored to preclude deterioration; computer records require a backup; quality records are kept a minimum of seven (7) years.

18.0 Internal Quality Audits

18A POLICY

An internal auditing system verifies the effectiveness and compliance of each Quality System element. Audits are performed to checklists by qualified, independent personnel, with additional audits when there is a significant change, adverse trends, a need to verify corrective action, or as required by the customer or management.

18B PROCEDURE — QAP-18, INTERNAL QUALITY AUDITS

1.0 Purpose

1.1 To verify the effective implementation, adequacy, and effectiveness of the Quality Assurance System.

2.0 Scope

2.1 Examination and evaluation of all system elements, including quality records.

3.0 Responsibilities

3.1 The Quality Manager is responsible for audit scheduling, planning, auditor training, corrective-action follow-up, and accepting the completed audit by signing the Final Audit Report.

4.0 General

4.1 The internal audit provides a comprehensive review of the Quality Assurance System and supports continual improvement.

5.0 Procedure

5.1 The Quality System audit is conducted typically during December; each element is audited at least once per year.

5.2 A separate annual ESD audit of the facility is conducted, with findings reported to the President.

5.3 Auditors do not audit areas for which they have direct responsibility when feasible; the Quality Manager assures auditor qualification.

5.4 Each audit follows an Audit Plan and Checklist, using NCRs to record deficiencies; a Team Leader is designated when multiple auditors participate.

5.5 At conclusion, an Audit Report and data are submitted to the Quality Manager, who determines and requests corrective action with a defined response time.

5.6 The Quality Manager verifies (re-audits) and signs off corrective actions and forwards audit data to the President for the annual Management Review.

19.0 End-of-Life & Obsolescence Control

19A POLICY

BNC controls the identification, phase-out, and communication of End-of-Life (EOL) and obsolete products, components, and materials throughout their lifecycle, ensuring continued traceability, timely customer notification, Last-Time-Buy (LTB) management, and controlled disposition in accordance with the Quality System. EOL status may be triggered by supplier or manufacturer discontinuation, component obsolescence, regulatory or compliance changes, engineering redesign, reliability or safety considerations, or internal determination by BNC management. All EOL actions are documented and retained as Quality Assurance Records per QAP-17.

19B PROCEDURE — QAP-19, END-OF-LIFE & OBSOLESCENCE CONTROL

1.0 Purpose

1.1 To define the process for identifying, managing, documenting, and communicating End-of-Life (EOL) status for BNC products, components, and materials, ensuring continued traceability, customer notification, and controlled phase-out.

2.0 Scope

2.1 All BNC-manufactured products, distributed products, assemblies, subassemblies, and purchased components that reach end-of-life or become obsolete. Applies to Operations, Engineering, Purchasing, Quality Assurance, and Customer Support.

3.0 General

BNC controls identification and traceability of items throughout their lifecycle (see QAP-08). The EOL process expands these controls to include formal phase-out, customer communication, and documentation. EOL status may be triggered by:

Supplier or manufacturer discontinuation

Component obsolescence

Regulatory or compliance changes

Engineering redesign

Reliability or safety considerations

Internal determination by BNC management

4.0 Procedure

4.1 Identification of EOL Items

4.1.1 Purchasing, Engineering, or Operations notifies Quality Assurance when an item is identified as obsolete or discontinued.

4.1.2 Quality Assurance verifies traceability to affected Sales Orders, inventory, and assemblies using existing identification controls (QAP-08).

4.2 EOL Review and Approval

4.2.1 Quality Assurance initiates an EOL Review to determine impact on active contracts, inventory, and customer commitments.

4.2.2 The Quality Manager approves the EOL classification and required actions, including replacement-part qualification or redesign.

4.3 Customer Notification

4.3.1 Customer Support issues formal EOL notifications when required by contract or when customer systems may be affected.

Notifications include the EOL effective date, Last-Time-Buy (LTB) availability, recommended replacement items, and support/service timelines.

4.4 Inventory and Material Disposition

4.4.1 Remaining inventory is allocated to existing Sales Orders, reserved for service/support, or scrapped if no longer usable.

4.4.2 Disposition is recorded using nonconformance or material-control documentation as applicable (QAP-15).

4.5 Documentation Requirements

EOL actions generate Quality Assurance Records, including the EOL Review Form, supplier discontinuation notices, customer notifications, replacement-part qualification, and inventory-disposition records, retained a minimum of seven (7) years per QAP-17.

4.6 Post-EOL Support

4.6.1 BNC continues to support EOL products for the duration specified by contract or internal policy.

4.6.2 Service parts are tracked using the same identification and traceability controls defined in QAP-08.

5.0 Responsibilities

Quality Manager — approves EOL classification; ensures documentation and record retention; coordinates cross-department actions.

Purchasing — obtains supplier discontinuation notices; identifies replacement components; updates the AVL if supplier changes are required.

Engineering — evaluates technical impact; approves replacement parts or redesigns.

Operations — manages inventory disposition; ensures segregation of obsolete items.

Customer Support — issues customer notifications; coordinates Last-Time-Buy communication.

6.0 Records

6.1 All EOL records are maintained as Quality Assurance Records per QAP-17, including EOL Review Forms, supplier notices, customer notifications, and disposition logs.
